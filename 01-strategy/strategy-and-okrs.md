# Product Strategy & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | "The platform crews choose and contractors can’t run without."  Meridian becomes the system field teams reach for first and enterprise leaders trust most, giving general contractors of every size, from $5M projects to the largest firms in the country, the visibility to win more work, run it profitably, and grow with confidence. |
| **Where to play** | Where we will focus: We will win the field on the projects we already run, then use that field experience to win the mid-market. Our first focus is the superintendents, foremen, and subcontractor crews working on projects for our existing top-100 general contractor customers, starting with the daily workflows they now handle by phone camera and group text. Once field adoption is proven there, we will promote our new field experience to land and expand $5M–$50M contractors into the full platform.  What we will leave out: We will not focus on contractors in the smallest segment (under $5M). We will not create a stand-alone field app disconnected from the core platform and data model. |
| **How to win** | Meridian is built to handle complexity and has the workflows the field already feeds. Every RFI, issue, and document the field creates already ends up in Meridian but gets there indirectly. Our advantage is that the workflows, approvers, and records the field depends on already live in our platform. We win by giving the field a direct way in to those workflows. |
| **Capabilities required** | Field discovery: Ongoing research on job sites to find which group-text and photo habits to replace first, and what benefits will drive field behavior. Capture that’s as easy as a text: Mobile capture (photo, voice, quick note) that works with the way field teams work so it’s as fast as a group text. Routing into existing workflows: Turning field input into the platform’s existing objects (RFIs, issues, documents, daily logs) automatically. Closing the loop with the field: Sending answers, approvals, and status back to the crew on mobile, so the field gets benefit in return. Field adoption through customer success: A playbook for rolling out to field teams on live projects and onboarding for future projects. |
| **Management systems** | Field engagement (monthly): Monthly field retention on active projects, and the share of field updates that start in Meridian rather than arriving by text or WhatsApp. Workflow impact (monthly): RFI turnaround and issue resolution time on projects with field adoption compared with projects without it. Enterprise revenue health (quarterly): Gross and net revenue retention across the top customers, and expansion tied to field adoption (seats, projects, renewals). Mid-market traction (quarterly): New $5M–$50M logos landed with the field offering, and churn in that segment. Quarterly field review: Product, UX research, and CS go over field feedback and adoption data from live projects with two or three customer GCs and decide what to maintain, adapt, or stop. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not invest in new enterprise analytics or ERP integrations beyond what existing contracts require. Rationale: Existing features are maintained so enterprise contracts are protected, but new investment goes to the field so we can move more quickly to close gaps and prevent churn and revenue loss.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Meridian the default way field teams capture and share their project work.
>
> - **KR1:** Monthly field retention on active projects reaches 60% across pilot customers.
> - **KR2:** 50% of field updates on pilot projects (photos, issues, RFIs, daily logs) start in Meridian rather than arriving by text or WhatsApp.
> - **KR3:** RFI turnaround time on projects with field adoption is 30% faster than on comparable projects without it.

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** My original hard no was positioned as "not pursue under-$5M contractors." AI evaluation called out that this doesn’t cost me anything, because no stakeholders were arguing for that segment. It suggested a real hard no would settle an internal debate.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** What I changed: Updated the "Hard No" statement to be more of a high stakes decision; created stronger connection in the Winning Aspiration between the experience and the value to the customer; refined the OKRs to ensure they are outcomes What I defended: Maintaining the overall value proposition of the product by balancing delivering value to users (field teams) without overemphasizing them at the expense of our buyers or other user segments.
