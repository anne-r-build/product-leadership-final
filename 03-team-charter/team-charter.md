# Team Charter: Meridian Foundations

> Module 3 · Set the Habits That Make a Team Faster

## 1. What We Own

> **We own:** The Foundations field experience from start to finish: offline-first mobile capture, the simplified daily log, AI-assisted RFI drafting, close-the-loop notifications, and routing field input into the core platform's existing objects, all measured against KR1–KR3. We also own the engineering capacity reserved for those bets, which is 70% of mobile engineering, fixed at quarterly planning and not available for other work.
>
> **Out of scope:** New enterprise analytics, ERP integrations, and renewal-driven feature requests belong to PM 2 and the enterprise team, and they're funded from enterprise capacity, never from Foundations' reserved capacity. The core platform's RFI, compliance, document control, and budget features stay with the core platform owners: we write field data into them but don't change how they work for finance and compliance users. Pricing and contract terms belong to sales and finance, with Foundations providing the adoption and value data.
>
> **Joint calls:** Any proposal to move engineers off a Now bet onto enterprise or renewal work needs a joint call between the Foundations lead, PM 1, and PM 2 before anyone tells a customer yes. So do any change to the shared data model or to a workflow that finance and compliance teams rely on, and any commitment sales wants to make in a contract that involves field features (such as time tracking).

## 2. How We Decide

> **Who decides scope:** PM 1 decides feature and scope calls within the field bets, and PM 2 decides them within enterprise, each using the KR they own to break ties. The Foundations lead decides how capacity is split between the two at quarterly planning, and that split holds for the quarter. Sales can request roadmap items, but it can't promise one to a customer without the owning PM's written sign-off.
>
> **How cross-team conflicts escalate:** Either PM raises a conflict within two business days, in a one-page note covering the request, the engineering cost, the impact on KRs, and the renewal revenue at risk; the PMs have 48 hours to resolve it, and if they can't, the Foundations lead decides within three business days. If a renewal is critical and keeping it would mean breaking the reserved field capacity, the VP of Product and the CRO make the final call within five business days, and until they decide, field capacity stays where it is. The 70% split and the timeframes are illustrative. What matters is the principle: field capacity is reserved up front, and it doesn't move until a named person makes the call. That answers "who protects the field bet?" The Foundations lead does, and protecting it is the default.
